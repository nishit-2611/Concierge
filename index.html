    Module 1 · 2 min

    What you're rating

    Real users asked an AI assistant for help. You read two of those conversations and judge how well each assistant helped. You don't chat with any AI yourself.

    Each task gives you

    • The user's verbatim first prompt, plus any follow-ups.
    • Assistant A's full conversation.
    • Assistant B's full conversation, answering the same first request.

    You deliver

    • Ratings and short explanations for A.
    • The same ratings for B.
    • A side-by-side verdict, backed by specific differences.
    Your seat

    Put yourself in the user's position. Read as if you were the person who needed this help, and answer every question from that perspective.

    What you'll see

    • Two transcripts labeled Assistant A and Assistant B in a plain, neutral layout. Which one comes first is randomized across raters.
    • Both are edited the same way: personal info masked, assistant names replaced with [assistant], and "which model version are you?" exchanges removed. Don't count these edits against either assistant.
    • A and B can have different numbers of turns (1 to 6+), because the user followed up differently.
    • The first prompt may differ trivially between A and B (capitalization, spacing, a typo). Treat it as the same request.
    • If you get source links instead of transcripts, assistant branding may be visible. Don't let it affect your ratings.
    • Some task IDs are skipped. Those tasks are on hold and come in a later batch.

    Module 2 · 2 min

    Five ground rules

    These apply to every task and both assistants.

    Read onlyZero lookups

    Judge only what's on the page. Don't cook the recipe, watch the show or play the album. Don't search Google, AllRecipes, IMDb, Netflix, Reddit or Spotify to check quantities, cooking science or where something streams.

    Same yardstickConsistency

    Apply the same definitions and anchors to every task and to both assistants.

    A, then BNo going back

    Finish every item for Assistant A before you read B. Once you reach the side-by-side, don't change individual ratings to match your overall pick.

    Substance over polishLength isn't quality

    Length, heavy formatting, dense bullets and upbeat phrasing don't make a better answer. Ask whether it respected the user's constraints and gave complete, coherent, plausible advice.

    Whole threadMulti-turn

    Conversations run from 1 turn to 6+. Judge how well the assistant handled the opening request and every follow-up on the way to the final answer.

    Module 3 · 1 min

    Workflow, step by step

    Do every task in this order. Don't skip ahead.

    1. Read the user prompt

      Note the domain (Recipe, or Entertainment: TV show / album). List every explicit constraint: dish type, 30–60 min weeknight time, diet, ingredients on hand, mood or vibe, runtime, streaming preference.

    2. Read all of Assistant A

      Top to bottom, including how it handled each follow-up.

    3. Score Assistant A right away

      While it's fresh.

    4. Read all of Assistant B

      Top to bottom.

    5. Score Assistant B on the same items
    6. Side-by-side

      Pick which assistant did better (SxS7) and cite the observable differences behind it (SxS8).

    7. Answer MC1 and MC2

      If the language in Assistant A's or B's conversation feels similar to an AI assistant you know, select that assistant from the options (ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude or Other). If it doesn't sound like any of them, select "No idea". Answer MC1 for A and MC2 for B.

    Module 4 · 1 min

    The severity scale

    Most items use these three labels. The domain anchors in the next two modules add detail on top of them.

    No Issue

    Fully delivers what this item asks, with no noticeable omissions, inaccuracies or friction.

    Minor Issue

    One or two small things are imperfect or slightly incomplete, but the user could still use it as-is or with trivial common-sense adjustments.

    Major Issue

    Significant omissions, contradictions or flaws on this item that undermine its usefulness or fail the user's request.

    Quick test

    Could the user still use it with a small, obvious tweak? That's Minor. Does the problem break the answer or the request? That's Major.

    Module 5 · 4 min

    Recipes rubric

    Users asked for a weeknight dinner main dish, roughly 30–60 minutes. Rate A on R-Q1 to R-Q9, then B on R-Q11 to R-Q19.

    TASKS R_01–R_249 items per assistant: 6 ratings, 3 short texts

    Followed the request

    A · R-Q1B · R-Q11Single select

    "Did it follow the request? Did the assistant give the user what they asked for? Did it respect their constraints (if any)?"

    No Issue

    Delivers the requested dish type and honors every stated diet, ingredient and time constraint.

    Minor Issue

    Mostly matches but drifts on a secondary detail, e.g. total time slightly over the limit.

    Major Issue

    Ignores an explicit restriction (meat or dairy when told not to) or gives a different type of dish.

    A · R-Q2B · R-Q12Open text · 1–3 sentences · required

    "Describe how well it followed the request. If there were any issues, what did it miss or get wrong? If no issues, what did it get right?"

    Cite the specific constraints from the prompt and the turn(s) that met or missed them.

    Complete

    A · R-Q3B · R-Q13Single select

    "Was it complete? Does the recipe include everything needed to actually cook this — all ingredients with amounts, every step, times and temperatures?"

    No Issue

    Every ingredient has a quantity, every step is there, and heat levels, times and temperatures are clear.

    Minor Issue

    Misses a minor detail, e.g. no amount for oil or salt, or one small prep step left implicit.

    Major Issue

    Leaves out key ingredient amounts, essential steps, or critical times or temperatures.

    A · R-Q4B · R-Q14Open text · required

    "What, if anything, was missing or incomplete?"

    Name unquantified ingredients, missing steps, or missing temperatures and times. If nothing is missing, write Complete.

    Easy to follow

    A · R-Q5B · R-Q15Single select

    "Was it easy to follow? Well-organized, logical order, easy to scan while cooking?"

    No Issue

    Ingredient list kept separate from numbered steps in cooking order. Easy to read at a glance.

    Minor Issue

    Understandable, but cluttered, dense paragraphs, or slightly awkward order (prep hidden in a later step).

    Major Issue

    Wall of text, steps out of cooking order, or a confusing structure.

    Tone

    A · R-Q6B · R-Q16Single select

    "Did the tone feel right? Natural, helpful, not robotic or overly chatty?"

    No Issue

    Warm, practical and natural for everyday cooking help.

    Minor Issue

    Slightly stiff, formulaic or over-the-top ("What a fantastic culinary journey!").

    Major Issue

    Robotic, condescending, preachy, or buried in filler.

    Trustworthy

    A · R-Q7B · R-Q17Single select

    "Did it seem trustworthy? Any weird ingredient combos, suspicious quantities, things that don't sound right?"

    Judge from the text alone. Don't test it or look it up.

    No Issue

    Proportions, flavor pairings, cook times and temperatures all look plausible.

    Minor Issue

    Something looks slightly off on paper, e.g. a heavy spice ratio or an optimistic cook time.

    Major Issue

    Implausible quantities, broken cooking logic or contradictions, e.g. an ingredient used in the steps but never listed, or raw pasta simmered for 2 minutes.

    A · R-Q8B · R-Q18Open text · required

    "Did anything about this recipe feel off or make you skeptical?"

    Point to the specific quantities, times, temperatures or steps. If nothing, write Looks trustworthy.

    Helpful extras

    A · R-Q9B · R-Q19Single select · 3 options

    "Did it offer helpful extras? Substitution tips, serving suggestions, prep-ahead advice, etc. — without being asked?"

    Yes, helpful extrasIt added useful tips on its own: substitutions, sides, storage, scaling.
    No extras, didn't need anyThe recipe stands cleanly on its own.
    No extras, wish it hadIt lacked context that would have helped.

    Module 6 · 3 min

    Entertainment rubric

    Users asked for a TV show or a music album/EP that matches a mood, vibe or niche interest. Rate A on E-Q1 to E-Q5 and E-Q9, then B on E-Q11 to E-Q15 and E-Q19.

    TASKS E_01–E_486 items per assistant: 4 ratings, 2 short texts. There is no E-Q6 to E-Q8; the gap is intentional.

    Understood the request

    A · E-Q1B · E-Q11Single select

    "Did it understand what the user wanted? Did the assistant recommend something that matches the vibe/mood described?"

    No Issue

    Clearly fits the mood, genre, format (TV show vs. album) and the stated constraints.

    Minor Issue

    Right general genre but misses a secondary nuance, e.g. episode length or tone.

    Major Issue

    Misses the requested vibe or format, or ignores an explicit exclusion.

    A · E-Q2B · E-Q12Open text · 1–3 sentences · required

    "How well did it match the request? If there were issues, what did it get wrong? If no issues, what did it nail?"

    Say specifically how the recommended title(s) and description fit or missed the prompt.

    Explained why

    A · E-Q3B · E-Q13Single select

    "Did it explain WHY this is a good match? Did it connect the recommendation back to what the user asked for?"

    No Issue

    Specific, tailored reasons tied directly to the user's stated mood or examples.

    Minor Issue

    Generic praise or a plot summary with no clear link to the user's vibe.

    Major Issue

    No reasoning, or filler that could apply to any show or album.

    A · E-Q4B · E-Q14Open text · required

    "What reasoning did it give?"

    Summarize the key reason it gave for why the pick fits. If none, write No reasoning provided.

    Enough info to find it

    A · E-Q5B · E-Q15Single select

    "Did it give enough info to find it? Which streaming service, how many episodes, runtime, artist name, album title?"

    No Issue

    Has what you need to start watching or listening: exact title, platform or artist, format and length.

    Minor Issue

    Omits one or two helpful details, e.g. title and plot but no streaming service or episode length.

    Major Issue

    Leaves out critical identifying or access information.

    Tone

    A · E-Q9B · E-Q19Single select

    "Did the tone feel right? Like a knowledgeable friend, not a sales pitch or a robot?"

    No Issue

    Conversational, natural and credible.

    Minor Issue

    Slightly stiff, encyclopedia-like, or overly promotional.

    Major Issue

    Robotic, template-driven or unnatural.

    Module 7 · 2 min

    Side-by-side and identity checks

    Only after both assistants are fully scored. Same items for both domains, prefixed R- or E-.

    R-SxS7 / E-SxS75-point scale

    "Overall Side-by-Side Preference: Which Assistant performed better overall on the task?"

    A much betterAssistant A was much better
    A betterAssistant A was better
    About the sameAssistant A and B were about the same
    B betterAssistant B was better
    B much betterAssistant B was much better
    R-SxS8 / E-SxS8Open text · 2–3 sentences · required

    "Which specific, observable differences between the two conversations drove your overall rating?"

    Recipes: cite exact ingredients, quantities, steps, times or turns.

    Entertainment: cite exact titles, metadata given (platform, length, release year), reasoning offered, or turns.

    Never mention your own taste, how you think the dish would turn out, or how much you think the user would enjoy it.

    Weak

    "B was better because it sounded tastier and was more detailed."

    Strong

    "A gave amounts for every ingredient and a 425°F, 20-minute bake. B listed 'spices' with no quantities and dropped the toddler portion the user asked for in turn 2."

    SxS1 to SxS6 were retired in an earlier version, so numbering starts at 7 on purpose.
